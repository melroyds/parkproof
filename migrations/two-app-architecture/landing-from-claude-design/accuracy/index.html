Accuracy

How we test ParkProof's sign reading.

ParkProof uses an AI model to read parking signs. To find out how often it gets them right, we run it over photos of real signs that a person has already read and labelled, and count the mistakes. This page explains the method, what each number means, and what the test can't tell you.

The method

Labelled photos, held out.

  1. Photograph real signs.We collect photos of real parking signs, including stacked poles, arrows, clearways, permit zones and paid parking. Number plates and faces are kept out of frame.
  2. Label each photo by hand.A person reads the sign and writes down the right answer for a fixed date and time: whether you can park, when you'd need to leave, how many minutes that is, and what the sign says about payment and permits. When the answer depends on which side of the pole you're on, the label says which side.
  3. Hold a set back.Each photo goes into one of two sets when it's added, before it's ever run. The development set is used while we improve the instructions the model follows. The test set never is. Only test set numbers appear on this page, so the model hasn't been tuned to the photos it's scored on. Which test photos it got wrong isn't published.
  4. Run the app's own code.Each photo goes through the same sign-reading code the app uses, with the clock set to the labelled date and time, so every run asks the same question.
  5. Count every read.The model can answer differently from one run to the next, so each photo can be read several times. Errors count as wrong answers.

The labelling rules and the test code are public: read the labelling rules.

Definitions

What each number means.

You can park
In this test, "you can park" means a driver who meets the sign's conditions can park there, for example by paying, or by holding the right permit for a permit zone or an accessible parking bay. A read that misses a payment or permit condition is counted under sign details. A leave-by time that's too late is counted under leave-by time.
Answer right
The can-park answer matches the label, and so does the leave-by time, to within a minute. When the label says there's no time to leave by, the answer must not give one.
Said you can park when you can't
Of the signs where you can't park at the labelled time, the share of reads that said you can. It's one of the mistakes that can cost you a fine, so it's reported on its own. Missing a payment or permit condition and giving a leave-by time that's too late can cost you a fine too. They have their own rows.
Said you can't park when you can
Of the signs where you can park, the share of reads that said you can't. This mistake can send you looking for another spot when you didn't need to.
Leave-by time right, minutes left right
Among reads that rightly said you can park, the share whose leave-by time, or number of minutes you can stay, is within a minute of the label. This includes signs where the label says there's no time to leave by. There the read must not give one.
Leave-by time later than the label
Among the same reads, the share whose leave-by time was later than the label's, or missing when the label has one. Staying past the real leave-by time can cost you a fine.
Sign details
Whether the read picked up what the sign says about paying, permit zones and areas, accessible parking bays and area signs. Only details the label states are counted.
Answer right by confidence
The model says how confident it is in each read. These rows show whether its high-confidence answers really are right more often.
95% range
A Wilson score interval. With a few dozen signs, the true rate is likely to be somewhere in this range, but it could be outside it. Ranges are counted per sign, not per read, because repeat reads of the same photo aren't independent.
Time per read and cost
p50 is the time half of all reads finish within, and p90 the time 90% finish within, measured on the test machine rather than your phone. Cost is the model's list price for the tokens each read used.
Limits

What the test can't tell you.

  • A few dozen photos can't cover every sign in Australia. The ranges give a rough idea of how much the numbers could change with other photos.
  • The test set may not include signs like yours, or signs from your city or state. When results are shown, the tags listed with them say what the test set covers.
  • Your photo may be darker, blurrier, more angled or more blocked than the photos in the test set, and harder to read.
  • A label is a person's reading of the sign. Labels can be wrong, and a council may read a sign differently.
  • The numbers apply to one model, set of instructions and app version, named with the results. A change to any of them needs a fresh run.
  • Numbers about many signs don't tell you whether one particular read is right. Always check the actual sign before you leave the car.
